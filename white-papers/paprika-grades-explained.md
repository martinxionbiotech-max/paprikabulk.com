# Paprika Grades Explained: A Complete Guide to Selecting the Right Quality

## 1. Introduction

Paprika grading is the system that classifies products by quality parameters — primarily ASTA color value, particle size, moisture, and purity. Understanding the grading system enables buyers to select the right product for their specific application while optimizing cost.

This guide explains the Dinweys four-grade system and how it maps to global market requirements.

---

## 2. The Four-Grade Classification

### Grade Overview Table

| Parameter | Premium | Superior | Standard | Special (ASTA200+) |
|-----------|---------|----------|----------|-------------------|
| ASTA Value | 160–200 | 120–160 | 80–120 | 200+ |
| Relative Price | ★★★★ | ★★★ | ★★ | ★★★★★ |
| Typical Customer | Large food processors | Mid-size manufacturers | Cost-sensitive buyers | Oleoresin/extraction |

### Premium Grade (ASTA 160–200)

**Target Market:** Premium sausage manufacturers, high-color applications, quality-focused food processors.

**Characteristics:**
- Deep red color with high coloring power
- Requires less product per batch to achieve target color
- Best for applications where visual appearance is critical

**Typical Specifications:**
- ASTA: 160 minimum
- Moisture: ≤10%
- Particle Size: 95% through 40 mesh
- SHU: ≤500 (sweet)

### Superior Grade (ASTA 120–160)

**Target Market:** General food manufacturing, seasoning blends, meat processing.

**Characteristics:**
- Good red color with reliable consistency
- Cost-effective for most applications
- Balances quality and price

### Standard Grade (ASTA 80–120)

**Target Market:** Cost-sensitive applications, B2B bulk supply, further processing.

**Characteristics:**
- Moderate color suitable for general use
- Most economical option
- May require higher usage rates

### Special ASTA200+ Grade

**Target Market:** Oleoresin extraction, premium export, maximum color applications.

**Characteristics:**
- Highest ASTA values available
- Limited availability (premium pricing)
- Preferred raw material for oleoresin production

## 4. Factors Beyond Grade

### When to Consider Beyond Grade

| Factor | Impact | Also Check |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Particle Size | Fine vs. coarse affects dispersion | Mesh specification |
| Pungency | Sweet vs. mildly hot | SHU or capsaicin content |
| Origin | Xinjiang vs. Gansu vs. blended | Certificate of origin |
| Sterilization | Steam vs. irradiated | Microbial specs |
| Certification | Organic, Kosher | Certification documents |

### Blending for Custom Specifications
Dinweys offers custom blending between grades to meet specific ASTA targets. For example, blending Premium (ASTA 180) and Standard (ASTA 100) at a 60:40 ratio produces a product of approximately ASTA 148 — which may be ideal for a specific application.

## 5. How to Select the Right Grade

### Decision Flowchart

1. **What is your application?**
   - Sausage/meat processing → Premium or Superior
   - Seasoning blends → Superior or Standard
   - Oleoresin extraction → Special ASTA200+
   - Cost-sensitive bulk → Standard

2. **What color intensity do you need?**
   - High visual impact → ASTA 160+
   - Moderate color → ASTA 120–160
   - General use → ASTA 80–120

3. **What volume do you need?**
   - Bulk container (20 MT+) → Any grade available
   - Sample or small batch → Minimum order applies

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I get a grade between Premium and Superior?**
A: Yes. We offer custom-specified products to meet specific ASTA targets. Contact our sales team with your requirements.

**Q: Does higher grade always mean better value?**
A: Not necessarily. Higher ASTA means you use less product, but at a higher per-kg price. Calculate your cost per unit of color — not per kg — to determine value.

**Q: Are grade names standardized across the industry?**
A: No. "Premium" from one supplier may be "Standard" from another. Always verify by ASTA value, not grade name alone.
