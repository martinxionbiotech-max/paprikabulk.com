# The Paprika Supply Chain: From Field to Factory to Port

## 1. Introduction

The journey of paprika from a fresh red pepper in the field to a standardized bulk ingredient arriving at your facility is complex. Each stage in the supply chain affects quality, cost, and delivery timelines.

This guide maps the complete paprika supply chain, highlighting critical quality control points and procurement considerations at each stage.

---

## 2. Supply Chain Overview

```
Farm (Growing & Harvest)
    ↓
Collection & Preliminary Sorting
    ↓
Drying (Sun or Mechanical)
    ↓
Threshing (Seed & Stem Removal)
    ↓
Storage (Whole Pods)
    ↓
Grinding & Sieving
    ↓
Blending (Optional)
    ↓
Quality Control Testing
    ↓
Packaging
    ↓
Container Loading
    ↓
Ocean Freight
    ↓
Destination Customs Clearance
    ↓
Warehouse / Final Delivery
```

## 3. Stage 1: Growing & Harvest

### Timeline
- **Planting:** April–May (Northern Hemisphere)
- **Growing:** May–September (120–150 days)
- **Harvest:** September–October

### Critical Quality Factors

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Variety selection | Determines ASTA potential |
| Irrigation management | Water stress → higher color concentration |
| Pest control | Pesticide residues affect export compliance |
| Harvest timing | Full ripeness = maximum ASTA |
| Weather at harvest | Rain delays harvest, can cause mold |

### Procurement Consideration
One crop per year in Northern Hemisphere. Plan procurement to cover full annual requirements. Post-harvest pricing is lowest (November–January).

## 4. Stage 2: Drying

### Methods

| Method | Time | Cost | Quality Impact |
|--------|------|------|----------------|
| Sun drying | 7–15 days | Lowest | 30–50% ASTA loss possible |
| Hot air drying | 6–12 hours | Moderate | 10–20% ASTA loss |
| Controlled dehydration | 8–16 hours | Higher | 5–10% ASTA loss |

### QC Checkpoints
- Moisture content ≤10%
- Color uniformity
- No mold or off-odors

---

## 5. Stage 3: Processing (Grinding & Blending)

### Grinding
From whole dried pods to powder, particles are reduced to the specified mesh size.

| Mesh Size | Particle Opening | Application |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| 20 mesh | 840 µm | Coarse |
| 40 mesh | 420 µm | Standard |
| 60 mesh | 250 µm | Fine |
| 80 mesh | 177 µm | Very fine |
| 100 mesh | 149 µm | Ultrafine |

### Blending
Multiple batches may be blended to achieve consistent specifications:
- Color standardization (blend high + low ASTA)
- Pungency adjustment (blend sweet + hot)
- Origin balancing (blend regions for consistent profile)

### QC Checkpoints
- Particle size distribution
- Color value (ASTA)
- Moisture after grinding
- Metal detection

## 6. Stage 4: Quality Control

### Testing Protocol

| Test | Frequency | Method |
|------|-----------|--------|
| ASTA Color | Every batch | ASTA 20.1 |
| Moisture | Every batch | LOD 105°C |
| Particle Size | Every batch | Sieve analysis |
| Microbiology | Every batch | ISO methods |
| Heavy Metals | Periodic | ICP-MS |
| Pesticide Residue | Periodic | GC-MS/MS |
| Organoleptic | Every batch | Sensory panel |

## 7. Stage 5: Packaging & Container Loading

### Packaging Options

| Type | Capacity | Protection Level |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| PP woven bag | 25 kg | Basic |
| Kraft paper bag + PE liner | 25 kg | Good |
| Vacuum bag | 5–20 kg | Excellent |
| FIBC (super sack) | 500–1,200 kg | Bulk |

### Container Loading Best Practices
- Use food-grade containers (clean, dry, no odors)
- Place desiccants in containers for moisture control
- Avoid floor contact (use pallets)
- Stretch wrap pallets for unit stability

## 9. Risk Management in the Supply Chain

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Crop failure (weather) | Multi-region sourcing strategy |
| Price volatility | Forward contracting |
| Quality variability | Multi-tier specification system |
| Shipping delays | Buffer inventory, Incoterms selection |
| Regulatory changes | Regulatory monitoring service |

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How long does the full supply chain take from harvest to delivery?**
A: Typically 3–4 months for fresh crop: harvest (September) → processing (October–November) → shipping (December) → arrival (January).

**Q: How can I ensure supply chain transparency?**
A: Request:
- Batch-specific COAs
- Certificate of Origin
- Farm/cooperative traceability records
- Third-party audit reports
