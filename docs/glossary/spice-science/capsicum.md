# Capsicum

## Definition

Capsicum is a genus of flowering plants in the Solanaceae (nightshade) family, whose fruits are cultivated worldwide as vegetables, spices, and natural colorants. Paprika is produced from specific varieties of *Capsicum annuum*.

## Overview

The genus *Capsicum* includes all chili peppers and sweet peppers. Paprika (the spice) is made from selected *Capsicum annuum* varieties bred specifically for intense red color, mild pungency, and desirable drying characteristics.

## Technical Explanation

**Five Domesticated Species:**

| Species | Common Types | Pungency | Paprika Use |
|---------|-------------|----------|-------------|
| *C. annuum* | Bell pepper, paprika pepper, jalapeño | 0–50,000 SHU | Primary paprika source |
| *C. frutescens* | Tabasco, bird's eye | 30,000–100,000 SHU | Hot sauces only |
| *C. chinense* | Habanero, scotch bonnet | 100,000–3,000,000 SHU | Not used for paprika |
| *C. baccatum* | Aji amarillo | 5,000–30,000 SHU | South American cuisine |
| *C. pubescens* | Rocoto | 50,000–250,000 SHU | Andean cuisine |

**Paprika-Specific Cultivars:**
- Noble Sweet (Hungary) — deep red, low SHU, complex aroma
- Kalocsa varieties — traditional Hungarian paprika types
- Xinjiang long red (China) — high ASTA, mild heat
- California Wonder type — thick flesh, high yield

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Understanding the species and cultivar is essential for predicting color profile, pungency, drying yield, and optimal processing method. Product documentation should specify the variety where relevant.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can any red chili be used to make paprika?**
A: No. True paprika is made from specific *Capsicum annuum* cultivars bred for color intensity, flesh thickness, and drying characteristics. Other species produce different flavor and color profiles.

**Q: What distinguishes Hungarian paprika from Chinese paprika at the species level?**
A: Both are *Capsicum annuum*, but different cultivars. Hungarian varieties (e.g., Noble Sweet) are bred for zero pungency and complex aroma. Chinese varieties are bred for high ASTA color values and higher yields.
