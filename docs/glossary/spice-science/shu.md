# Scoville Heat Units (SHU)

## Definition

Scoville Heat Units (SHU) is the measurement of pungency (spiciness/heat) in chili peppers and paprika, quantifying the concentration of capsaicinoids — primarily capsaicin and dihydrocapsaicin.

## Overview

SHU measures the "hotness" of capsicum products. While paprika is generally a sweet (non-pungent) spice, some varieties carry mild heat. SHU helps buyers select the right pungency level for their application — from 0 SHU (completely sweet) to 500+ SHU (mildly hot).

## Technical Explanation

**Measurement Methods:**

| Method | Technique | Precision |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **HPLC (Modern Standard)** | Quantifies capsaicinoids directly | Most accurate |
| **Sensory Panel (Traditional)** | Dilution tasting by trained panelists | Subjective |
| **ASTA Pungency Method** | HPLC-based, results in SHU equivalent | Industry standard |

**SHU Scale for Paprika:**

| Type | SHU Range | Capsaicin (ppm) |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Sweet Paprika | 0–100 | 0–15 |
| Mild Paprika | 100–1,000 | 15–150 |
| Semi-Hot | 1,000–5,000 | 150–750 |
| Hot | 5,000–15,000 | 750–2,250 |

**Conversion:**
1 SHU ≈ 0.15 ppm capsaicin (approximate)
HPLC result (ppm capsaicinoids) × 15–16 = SHU

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

SHU is a critical specification parameter for paprika products. Sweet paprika (low SHU) is preferred for color applications, while mildly pungent varieties are used in certain ethnic cuisines. The COA should specify either SHU or capsaicin content.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do different origins produce different SHU levels?**
A: Yes. Chinese paprika from Xinjiang typically ranges 500–2,000 SHU (mildly pungent), while Hungarian sweet paprika is bred for 0–100 SHU.

**Q: Can SHU be reduced in processed paprika?**
A: Yes. Blending hot and sweet varieties, or using steam treatment, can reduce pungency. However, flavor profile may also change.
