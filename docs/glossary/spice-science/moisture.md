# Moisture Content

## Definition

Moisture content is the percentage of water present in paprika powder, typically measured by Loss on Drying (LOD) or Karl Fischer titration.

## Overview

Moisture content is a critical quality parameter for paprika. High moisture promotes microbial growth, caking, enzymatic degradation, and accelerated color loss. Industry standards typically require moisture ≤10–12%.

## Technical Explanation

**Measurement Methods:**

| Method | Principle | Time | Precision |
|--------|-----------|------|-----------|
| Loss on Drying (LOD) | Weight loss after heating at 105°C | 2–4 hours | ±0.5% |
| Karl Fischer | Direct water titration | 5–10 min | ±0.1% |
| Halogen Moisture Analyzer | Rapid drying with halogen heat | 5–15 min | ±0.2% |

**Impact of Moisture on Quality:**

| Moisture Level | Effect |
|---------------|--------|
| <8% | Excellent shelf stability, free-flowing |
| 8–10% | Good stability, standard quality |
| 10–12% | Acceptable, monitor during storage |
| >12% | Increased risk of caking, mold, degradation |

**Equilibrium Moisture:**
Paprika is hygroscopic — it absorbs or releases moisture to reach equilibrium with the ambient relative humidity. Storage in sealed packaging with desiccant is standard practice.

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Moisture content is a mandatory parameter on every COA and spec sheet. It directly affects product weight, pricing (water is not the product), shelf life, and shipping stability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why is moisture control important for long-distance shipping?**
A: Container shipping exposes paprika to temperature extremes and condensation. High moisture content plus temperature changes can lead to mold growth during transit.

**Q: Is paprika with 5% moisture better than 10%?**
A: Lower moisture improves stability, but extremely low moisture (<5%) is difficult to achieve economically and may indicate excessive heat during drying, which degrades color.
