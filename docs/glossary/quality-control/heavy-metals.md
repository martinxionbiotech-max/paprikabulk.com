# Heavy Metals

## Definition

Heavy metals testing measures the concentration of toxic metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury) in paprika products to ensure compliance with food safety regulations.

## Overview

Paprika, as an agricultural product grown in soil, can accumulate heavy metals from the environment. Import regulations in the EU, US, and other markets set maximum limits for heavy metals in spices.

## Technical Explanation

**Maximum Limits by Market (ppm):**

| Metal | EU Limit | US Limit | China Limit |
|-------|----------|----------|-------------|
| Lead (Pb) | ≤3.0 | ≤5.0 | ≤5.0 |
| Cadmium (Cd) | ≤0.3 | ≤0.5 | — |
| Arsenic (As) | ≤1.0 (inorganic) | ≤5.0 | ≤2.0 |
| Mercury (Hg) | ≤0.1 | — | ≤0.1 |

**Testing Methods:**
- ICP-MS (most sensitive and accurate)
- ICP-OES (alternative for higher detection limits)
- Atomic Absorption (traditional, less sensitive)

**Sources of Contamination:**
- Soil composition (natural background levels)
- Irrigation water quality
- Fertilizers and pesticides
- Processing equipment wear
- Storage container contamination

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Heavy metals testing is mandatory for EU and US import. The COA should include heavy metals results if the destination market requires them. Many buyers request heavy metals data as part of supplier qualification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why does the EU have stricter limits than the US?**
A: The EU operates on the ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principle for heavy metals, while US limits are based on risk assessments from the FDA.

**Q: Can heavy metals be removed from paprika?**
A: No practical method exists to remove heavy metals from the final product. Control must be at the raw material stage — selecting growing regions with low soil heavy metal content.
