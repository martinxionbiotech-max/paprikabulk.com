# HACCP

## Definition

HACCP (Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point) is a systematic, preventive approach to food safety that identifies physical, chemical, and biological hazards in production processes and establishes control measures at critical points.

## Overview

HACCP is the foundation of modern food safety management. It is a legal requirement for food processing facilities in most countries and is essential for paprika processors who supply international markets.

## Technical Explanation

**Seven HACCP Principles:**

| Principle | Description | Paprika Example |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| 1. Hazard Analysis | Identify potential hazards | Metal fragments from grinding, Salmonella from raw material |
| 2. Identify CCPs | Critical control points | Metal detection after grinding, steam sterilization |
| 3. Establish Critical Limits | Acceptable ranges | Metal detector: ≥1.0 mm ferrous = reject |
| 4. Monitor CCPs | Continuous or scheduled checks | Every 30 min metal detector test |
| 5. Corrective Actions | Fix deviations | Reprocess or reject contaminated batch |
| 6. Verification | Confirm system works | Quarterly HACCP audit |
| 7. Record Keeping | Document everything | Monitoring logs, corrective action reports |

**CCP Examples in Paprika Processing:**
- **CCP-1:** Raw material receiving (supplier COA verification)
- **CCP-2:** Metal detection after grinding
- **CCP-3:** Sterilization (if applied)
- **CCP-4:** Container loading hygiene

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

HACCP certification demonstrates a supplier's commitment to food safety. It is often a prerequisite for supplying major food manufacturers and is increasingly required for import in developed markets.
