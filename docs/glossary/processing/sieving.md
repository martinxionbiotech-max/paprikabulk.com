# Sieving

## Definition

Sieving is the process of separating ground paprika into different particle size fractions by passing it through screens (sieves) of specific mesh sizes.

## Overview

Sieving ensures that the final product meets the specified particle size distribution. It is typically performed after grinding to classify the powder and remove oversized particles.

## Technical Explanation

**Sieving Equipment:**

| Equipment | Mechanism | Capacity |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| Vibratory Sieve | Mechanical vibration | Medium-high |
| Rotary Sifter | Rotating cylindrical screen | High |
| Air-Jet Sieve | Air pulsing through screen | Low (analytical) |
| Ultrasonic Sieve | Ultrasonic vibration + mesh | Fine powders |

**Quality Control Parameters:**
- % through specified mesh (e.g., 95% through 40 mesh)
- % on specified mesh (oversized)
- % fines (sub-100 mesh)

**Typical Specifications:**
- 95% minimum through target mesh
- 100% maximum through next finer mesh
- No oversized particles

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Sieving specification (mesh size and passing percentage) is a critical product quality parameter documented on the spec sheet and verified during QC.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why is sieving important for industrial users?**
A: Consistent particle size ensures uniform color release, predictable flow properties in automated systems, and consistent visual appearance in finished products.

**Q: Can sieving be done without grinding?**
A: Flakes and coarse products are sieved to remove fines. Powder requires prior grinding.
