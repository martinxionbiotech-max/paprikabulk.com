# Grinding

## Definition

Grinding (milling) is the mechanical process of reducing dried paprika fruits into powder of specified particle size, typically using hammer mills, pin mills, or stone mills.

## Overview

Grinding transforms dried paprika into the final powder form. The grinding process must balance particle size reduction with heat management — excessive heat from grinding degrades color and can cause caking.

## Technical Explanation

**Mill Types:**

| Mill Type | Particle Range | Heat Generation | Application |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Hammer Mill | 20–80 mesh | Moderate | General purpose |
| Pin Mill | 40–120 mesh | Low | Fine powder |
| Stone Mill | 20–60 mesh | Low | Traditional, premium |
| Cryogenic Mill | 80–200 mesh | Very low (liquid N₂) | Ultrafine, heat-sensitive |

**Process Considerations:**
- Mill speed: Higher RPM = finer grind but more heat
- Feed rate: Controlled to prevent mill choking
- Temperature monitoring: Keep below 50°C to preserve color
- Sieving: Post-grinding classification to target mesh size
- Cooling: Air cooling or water-jacketed milling

**Yield Factors:**
- Whole pod → powder: 90–95% yield (loss = stem, seed fragments removed)
- Seed content affects oil content and shelf life

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Particle size (mesh) is a key specification parameter on the spec sheet. The grinding process must be matched to the required particle size — finer grinds require more energy and produce more heat, potentially affecting color.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why does paprika powder sometimes clump after grinding?**
A: Clumping is caused by:
- Residual moisture >10%
- Oil release from seeds (high temperature grinding)
- Static electricity (can be mitigated with anti-static equipment)

**Q: Can I request a custom particle size?**
A: Yes. Different applications require different particle sizes. Dinweys offers various mesh sizes from coarse (20 mesh) to ultrafine (100 mesh).
