# Microbiological Standards

## Definition

Microbiological standards are the specified limits for microorganisms (bacteria, yeast, mold, pathogens) in paprika products, ensuring safety and shelf stability.

## Overview

Dried spices like paprika are low-moisture foods, which generally inhibits microbial growth. However, pathogens can survive in dry conditions. Microbiological testing ensures that products meet safety requirements for the destination market.

## Technical Explanation

**Common Microbiological Specifications:**

| Parameter | EU Standard | US Standard | China Standard |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| Salmonella | Negative/25g | Negative/25g | Negative/25g |
| E. coli | <10 CFU/g | <10 CFU/g | <10 CFU/g |
| Yeast & Mold | <1,000 CFU/g | <10,000 CFU/g | <1,000 CFU/g |
| Aerobic Plate Count | <100,000 CFU/g | <100,000 CFU/g | <50,000 CFU/g |
| Coliforms | <100 CFU/g | <100 CFU/g | <100 CFU/g |
| Bacillus cereus | <1,000 CFU/g | — | <1,000 CFU/g |
| Staphylococcus aureus | <100 CFU/g | — | <100 CFU/g |

**Contamination Prevention:**
- HACCP-based production controls
- Steam sterilization (optional treatment)
- Irradiation (where permitted by regulation)
- Good manufacturing practices throughout processing

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Microbiological data is mandatory for import clearance in most countries. Failing microbiological standards can result in shipment rejection, destruction, or costly fumigation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can paprika be sterilized without affecting quality?**
A: Steam sterilization is effective but may reduce ASTA color value by 5–15%. Irradiation preserves color better but is not accepted in all markets (e.g., EU restrictions).

**Q: Is Salmonella naturally present in paprika?**
A: No. Salmonella is a contaminant from improper handling, contaminated water, or animal contact during production. Its presence indicates a GMP failure.
