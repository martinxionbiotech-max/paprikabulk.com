# Acceptance Criteria

## Definition

Acceptance criteria are the predefined limits (minimum, maximum, or range) that a test result must meet for the product to be considered acceptable and released for shipment.

## Overview

Acceptance criteria translate specifications into actionable pass/fail decisions. Each parameter on the specification has defined acceptance criteria that are compared against actual test results on the COA.

## Technical Explanation

**Types of Acceptance Criteria:**

| Type | Example | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| NLT (Not Less Than) | ASTA NLT 160 | ≥160 is acceptable |
| NMT (Not More Than) | Moisture NMT 10% | ≤10% is acceptable |
| Range | pH 4.5–6.0 | Between 4.5 and 6.0 acceptable |
| Qualitative | Appearance: Red powder | Visual match required |
| Negative | Salmonella: Negative/25g | Pathogen not detected |

**Decision Flow:**
1. Sample is tested using the specified method
2. Result is compared to the acceptance criterion
3. Within limit → Accept
4. Outside limit → Reject (or negotiate)

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Acceptance criteria appear on every COA and are the basis for quality decisions. Understanding them ensures correct product selection and prevents disputes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Are acceptance criteria the same for all countries?**
A: The criteria may vary. The EU, US (FDA), and China each have different regulatory limits for certain parameters (e.g., pesticide residues, aflatoxins). Always confirm the destination country's requirements.

**Q: What does "pass" mean on a COA?**
A: It means the test result meets the acceptance criterion for that parameter. Some COAs use "Conforms," "Complies," or "Within Specification."
