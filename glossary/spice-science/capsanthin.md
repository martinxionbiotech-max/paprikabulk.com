# Capsanthin

## Definition

Capsanthin is the primary carotenoid pigment in red paprika, responsible for the deep red color. It is a xanthophyll (oxygen-containing carotenoid) that constitutes 30–60% of total carotenoids in ripe paprika fruit.

## Overview

Capsanthin is the most important pigment molecule in paprika from a commercial perspective. Its concentration directly correlates with the ASTA color value. Paprika varieties bred for high color content are essentially selected for high capsanthin accumulation.

## Technical Explanation

**Chemical Properties:**
- Molecular formula: C₄₀H₅₆O₃
- Molecular weight: 584.85 Da
- Color: Intense red (λmax ~475 nm)
- Solubility: Fat-soluble (oils, organic solvents)
- Classification: Xanthophyll carotenoid

**Factors Affecting Capsanthin Accumulation:**
- Sunlight exposure: High light = more capsanthin
- Temperature: Optimal at 25–30°C during ripening
- Maturity: Peaks at full red ripeness
- Variety: Genetic capacity varies significantly

**Degradation:**
Capsanthin is more stable than beta-carotene but still susceptible to:
- Oxidation (primary degradation pathway)
- Light-induced isomerization
- Heat degradation above 60°C

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Capsanthin content is sometimes reported individually on advanced COAs. It provides a more precise quality indicator than ASTA alone, as it specifically measures the red pigment (not just total color).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is capsanthin the same as paprika oleoresin?**
A: No. Capsanthin is a specific pigment molecule. Oleoresin is the complete extract containing capsanthin, other carotenoids, lipids, and flavor compounds.

**Q: Why does some paprika appear orange rather than red?**
A: Orange paprika has a higher proportion of beta-carotene (orange) relative to capsanthin (red). This is variety-dependent and may also indicate that the paprika was harvested before full ripeness.
