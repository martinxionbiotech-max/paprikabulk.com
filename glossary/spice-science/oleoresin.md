# Oleoresin

## Definition

Oleoresin paprika (also called paprika extract or paprika oleoresin) is a concentrated, solvent-extracted form of the color and flavor compounds from paprika, used as a natural food colorant (E160c in the EU).

## Overview

Oleoresin is the most concentrated form of paprika color. It is produced by extracting ground paprika with a food-grade solvent (typically hexane or ethanol), then removing the solvent to leave a viscous, deep-red liquid containing carotenoid pigments and flavor compounds.

## Technical Explanation

**Production Process:**
1. Paprika powder (high ASTA, typically >200) is packed into extraction columns
2. Solvent percolates through the powder, dissolving pigments and oils
3. Miscella (solvent + extract) is collected
4. Solvent is removed by evaporation
5. Remaining oleoresin is standardized to specific color strength

**Typical Specifications:**

| Parameter | Typical Value |
|-----------|---------------|
| Color Strength | 40,000–150,000 ASTA units |
| Capsanthin Content | 5–20% |
| Viscosity | 500–5,000 cP |
| Solvent Residue | <30 ppm (EU), <25 ppm (US) |
| Appearance | Dark red viscous liquid |

**Applications:**
- Processed meats (sausages, salami)
- Snack foods (cheese coatings, seasonings)
- Beverages (natural red color)
- Cosmetics (lip colors)
- Feed additives (egg yolk color enhancement)

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Oleoresin specifications differ significantly from ground paprika. Color strength is expressed much higher (40,000+ ASTA units vs. 80–200 for powder). Documentation must specify the extraction solvent, color strength, and residual solvent levels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How much ground paprika does it take to make oleoresin?**
A: Approximately 10:1 to 20:1 ratio — 10–20 kg of high-ASTA paprika powder produces 1 kg of oleoresin, depending on the starting ASTA value.

**Q: Is oleoresin more cost-effective than powder?**
A: For large-scale industrial use, yes. Oleoresin eliminates storage of bulky powder, provides standardized color batch-to-batch, and has a much longer shelf life (2+ years vs. 12 months for powder).
