# Capsaicin

## Definition

Capsaicin (also known as capsaicinoids as a group) is the chemical compound responsible for the pungent (spicy/hot) sensation in chili peppers and paprika. It is an alkaloid produced in the placental tissue of the fruit.

## Overview

Capsaicinoids are the compounds that create the "heat" sensation when consuming spicy foods. In paprika, capsaicin content varies by variety, growing conditions, and processing. Sweet paprika has minimal capsaicin (0–0.01%), while hot varieties can contain significantly more.

## Technical Explanation

**Major Capsaicinoids:**

| Compound | % of Total | Pungency (SHU/mg) |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Capsaicin | 40–60% | ~16,000,000 |
| Dihydrocapsaicin | 30–50% | ~15,000,000 |
| Nordihydrocapsaicin | 5–10% | ~9,000,000 |
| Homocapsaicin | 1–5% | ~6,000,000 |
| Homodihydrocapsaicin | 1–5% | ~6,000,000 |

**Measurement Methods:**
- HPLC (highly accurate, modern standard)
- Sensory panel (traditional Scoville test)
- ASTA 21.0 method (HPLC-based capsaicinoid analysis)

**Factors Affecting Capsaicin Content:**
- Genetics (variety/cultivar)
- Growing temperature (hotter = more capsaicin)
- Water stress (reduced water = increased concentration)
- Maturity at harvest
- Drying process (heat can degrade capsaicinoids)

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Capsaicin content (or its SHU equivalent) is a critical specification parameter. Sweet paprika buyers require very low capsaicin (<0.01%). Understanding capsaicin levels helps match products to specific applications.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is capsaicin destroyed by heat during paprika processing?**
A: Partial degradation occurs during drying at high temperatures. Controlled drying (50–65°C) preserves most capsaicinoids while removing moisture effectively.

**Q: Can capsaicin levels be adjusted after processing?**
A: Yes. Hot and sweet paprika powders can be blended to achieve target pungency. This is a common industry practice for consistent product specifications.
