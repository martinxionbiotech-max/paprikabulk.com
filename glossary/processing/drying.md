# Drying

## Definition

Drying is the process of removing moisture from fresh paprika fruits to reduce water activity, preserve quality, and prepare the material for grinding or further processing.

## Overview

Drying is the most critical step in paprika processing. The method and temperature directly affect color retention (ASTA), flavor development, and final product quality. The goal is to reduce moisture from ~80% (fresh) to ≤10% (dried) as efficiently as possible while preserving quality.

## Technical Explanation

**Drying Methods:**

| Method | Temperature | Duration | Quality Impact |
|--------|-------------|----------|----------------|
| Sun Drying | Ambient | 7–15 days | Variable quality, UV degrades color |
| Hot Air Drying | 60–85°C | 6–12 hours | Fast, moderate color loss |
| Shade Drying | Ambient (shaded) | 10–20 days | Best color retention, slow |
| Dehydration (Controlled) | 50–65°C | 8–16 hours | Consistent quality, good retention |
| Freeze Drying | -20 to 40°C | 24–48 hours | Excellent quality, cost prohibitive |

**Key Parameters:**
- Inlet temperature: 55–85°C (higher = faster but more color loss)
- Final moisture target: ≤10% (typically 6–8%)
- Drying ratio: 5:1 to 8:1 (fresh → dried, depending on variety)

**Quality Considerations:**
- Temperatures >70°C accelerate carotenoid degradation
- Prolonged drying at low temperature may allow enzymatic browning
- Uniform drying requires proper fruit sizing and bed depth management

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Drying method and parameters affect specification compliance (moisture, ASTA retention). Documentation should note the drying method if it impacts quality characteristics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the best drying method for color retention?**
A: Controlled dehydration at 50–65°C with proper air flow provides the best balance of speed and color retention. Sun drying is cost-effective but can cause 30–50% ASTA loss.

**Q: Does drying affect pungency?**
A: Capsaicinoids are heat-stable, so drying has minimal effect on SHU values. However, prolonged high heat can slightly reduce capsaicinoid content.
