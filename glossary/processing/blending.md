# Blending

## Definition

Blending is the process of mixing two or more paprika batches — or paprika with other ingredients — to achieve consistent specifications for color, pungency, or other quality parameters.

## Overview

Blending is a critical tool for product standardization. Because natural agricultural products vary by harvest season, growing region, and variety, blending allows manufacturers to deliver consistent product specifications year-round.

## Technical Explanation

**Blending Applications:**

| Purpose | Example | Benefit |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Color Standardization | Blend ASTA 180 + ASTA 120 → ASTA 150 | Consistent color batch-to-batch |
| Pungency Adjustment | Blend sweet + hot to target SHU | Consistent heat level |
| Grade Optimization | Blend premium + standard to target grade | Cost optimization |
| Texture Control | Blend fine + coarse for target particle distribution | Consistent mouthfeel |
| Regional Characteristics | Blend Xinjiang + Gansu origins | Balanced flavor profile |

**Blending Ratio Calculation:**
Target Value = (Batch A Value × Ratio A) + (Batch B Value × Ratio B)

**Documentation:**
Each blended lot should have:
- Blend formula (component batches and ratios)
- Calculated target specifications
- Verified final test results (COA)

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Blending documentation is essential for traceability. The COA for a blended product should reference the component batches and the blend formula.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is blending considered adulteration?**
A: No. Blending is a standard industry practice for standardization. Adulteration refers to the addition of non-paprika materials (starch, red clay, artificial colors) to cheapen the product.
