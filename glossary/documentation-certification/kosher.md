# Kosher Certification

## Definition

Kosher certification verifies that paprika products meet Jewish dietary law (kashrut) requirements, ensuring that production, processing, and handling comply with kosher standards.

## Overview

Kosher certification is important for accessing Jewish and broader markets where kosher compliance is preferred. Many food manufacturers require kosher-certified ingredients to maintain their own kosher certification.

## Technical Explanation

**Kosher Requirements for Paprika:**

| Requirement | Implementation |
|-------------|---------------|
| Equipment | Dedicated or properly kosherized equipment |
| Ingredients | No non-kosher additives |
| Supervision | Periodic on-site inspection by kosher certifier |
| Labeling | Symbol of certifying agency on packaging |
| Production | No production during Passover (special handling) |

**Major Kosher Certifying Agencies:**
- OU (Orthodox Union) — most widely recognized
- Kof-K
- Star-K
- CRC
- OK Kosher

**Halal Certification:**
While distinct from kosher, halal certification is also commonly requested for paprika exports to Muslim-majority markets. Key requirements:
- No alcohol-based processing aids
- No cross-contamination with haram materials
- Halal slaughter compliance (not applicable for plant-based products)

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Kosher or halal certification documentation (certificate, scope, expiry) should accompany product documentation. Some buyers require kosher certification as a prerequisite.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is all paprika naturally kosher?**
A: Paprika (100% ground capsicum) is inherently kosher. However, equipment shared with non-kosher products, processing aids, or additives may affect kosher status. Certified kosher ensures complete compliance.

**Q: Does kosher certification expire?**
A: Yes. Kosher certificates typically have annual expiry dates. Always verify that the certification is current at the time of shipment.
