# Certificate of Analysis (COA)

## Definition

A Certificate of Analysis (COA) is an official document issued by the supplier that reports quality test results for a specific batch of paprika, confirming that the product meets agreed specifications.

## Overview

The COA is the most important quality document in paprika trade. It provides batch-specific evidence that the product meets its specifications for color (ASTA), moisture, particle size, microbiology, and other parameters.

## Technical Explanation

**Standard COA Sections:**

| Section | Information |
|---|---|
| Header | Company name, document ID, issue date |
| Product Info | Product name, grade, batch/lot number, quantity |
| Physical Tests | ASTA, moisture, particle size, appearance |
| Chemical Tests | Pungency (SHU), ash, heavy metals |
| Microbiological Tests | Salmonella, E. coli, yeast/mold, APC |
| Certifying Statement | "Pass" declaration, authorized signature |

**Verification Checklist:**
1. ✅ Batch number matches product label
2. ✅ All test results meet specification limits
3. ✅ Test methods are appropriate and cited
4. ✅ Document is properly signed and dated
5. ✅ ASTA value is within the expected range for the grade

## Importance in Paprika Documentation

Every shipment should include a batch-specific COA. Buyers should verify the COA upon receipt. Reputable suppliers (like Dinweys) provide COAs routinely with every shipment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Should I accept a generic COA instead of a batch-specific one?**
A: No. A generic COA does not represent the actual batch you are receiving. Always request the batch-specific COA.

**Q: What should I verify first on a paprika COA?**
A: First, confirm the batch number. Then check the ASTA value meets your specification. Finally, review microbiological results for your destination market.
